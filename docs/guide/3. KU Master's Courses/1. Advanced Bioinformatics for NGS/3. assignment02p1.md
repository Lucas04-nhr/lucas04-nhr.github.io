## Ancestry-Aware Genotype Calls for an Admixed Mexican Individual {#overview}

This analysis estimates ancestry proportions for NA19663, calls its 50,000 genotypes using four approaches, and compares the calls with SNP-chip genotypes. The four approaches are a uniform genotype prior, a Hardy-Weinberg prior based on the Asian/Native American ancestral component, an ancestry-weighted Hardy-Weinberg prior, and Beagle 3.3.2 imputation.

## Estimate ancestral frequencies and ancestry proportions {#ancestry-estimation}

NGSadmix was run on all 100 individuals and 50,000 SNPs using three ancestral components, no minor allele frequency filter, three threads, and random seed 1:

```bash
./NGSadmix -likes data/assignment02/input.gz \
           -K 3 -P 3 -minMaf 0 \
           -o output/assignment02/outK3 -s 1
```

The analysis converged after 218 iterations. Components were interpreted by comparing the population mean ancestry proportions. `outK3.qopt` column 1 was highest in CEU and was labelled European; column 3 was highest in YRI and was labelled African; and column 2 was highest in CHB and was labelled Asian/Native American. The Asian/Native American label is approximate: CHB identifies the component, but it is not a direct reference sample for Native American ancestry.

## Call genotypes with four approaches {#genotype-calling}

Let $j \in \{1,\ldots,50{,}000\}$ index sites, let $g \in \{0,1,2\}$ denote the genotype coded as the number of copies of the allele represented by the NGSadmix frequency, and let $L_{jg}=P(D_j\mid G_j=g)$ be the genotype likelihood for the sequencing data $D_j$. For a genotype prior $\pi_{jg}$, the posterior probability was calculated as

$$
P(G_j=g\mid D_j)=
\frac{L_{jg}\pi_{jg}}
{\sum_{h=0}^{2}L_{jh}\pi_{jh}}.
$$

At every site, the called genotype was the value of $g$ with the largest posterior probability, and call confidence was that largest posterior probability.

### Uniform genotype prior {#uniform-prior}

The uniform prior was

$$
\pi_{j0}=\pi_{j1}=\pi_{j2}=\frac{1}{3}.
$$

Because the three likelihoods in `input.gz` are already scaled to sum to one, the posterior probabilities under this prior are equal to the input likelihoods after normalization.

### Asian Native American Hardy Weinberg prior {#asian-prior}

Let $f_{jA}$ be the estimated frequency at site $j$ in the Asian/Native American component. Under Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, the genotype prior was

$$
\pi_{j0}=(1-f_{jA})^2,\qquad
\pi_{j1}=2f_{jA}(1-f_{jA}),\qquad
\pi_{j2}=f_{jA}^2.
$$

These prior probabilities were combined with the likelihoods using the posterior formula above.

### Ancestry weighted Hardy Weinberg prior {#ancestry-weighted-prior}

A prior tailored to NA19663 was constructed from all three ancestral components. Let $q_k$ be NA19663's estimated ancestry proportion for component $k\in\{1,2,3\}$, and let $f_{jk}$ be the estimated allele frequency at site $j$ in component $k$. The individual-specific allele frequency was

$$
p_j=\sum_{k=1}^{3}q_k f_{jk}.
$$

The genotype prior was then

$$
\pi_{j0}=(1-p_j)^2,\qquad
\pi_{j1}=2p_j(1-p_j),\qquad
\pi_{j2}=p_j^2.
$$

This prior is more closely matched to the estimated admixture of NA19663 than using only the Asian/Native American component because NA19663 has substantial contributions from more than one ancestral component. Weighting the site-specific frequencies by the individual's ancestry proportions allows both the European and Asian/Native American contributions, as well as the small African contribution, to influence the prior. This approximates both allele copies using the same ancestry-weighted frequency; it does not infer local ancestry.

### Beagle imputation {#beagle-imputation}

Beagle 3.3.2 was run on the likelihood data for all individuals:

```bash
java -Xmx4g -jar ./beagle.jar \
     like=data/assignment02/input.gz \
     out=output/assignment02/beagle
```

The three genotype probabilities for NA19663 in `beagle.input.gz.gprobs.gz` were normalized and used directly as posterior probabilities for calling.

## Evaluate discordance and missingness {#evaluation}

The 156 sites with a missing chip genotype for NA19663 were excluded, leaving 49,844 comparable sites. The confidence distributions above include all 50,000 sites, whereas the evaluation below excludes missing chip genotypes. For a posterior threshold $t$, a genotype call was retained if its called-genotype posterior probability was at least $t$. Missingness and discordance were calculated as

$$
\text{missingness}(t)=1-\frac{N_{\mathrm{retained}}(t)}{49{,}844},
$$

$$
\text{discordance}(t)=
\frac{N_{\mathrm{discordant,retained}}(t)}{N_{\mathrm{retained}}(t)}.
$$

### Discordance versus missingness {#discordance-plot}

![Discordance against missingness as the posterior threshold changes.](https://pic.lucas04.top/1790235890609.jpg)

Beagle had the lowest discordance at zero missingness and generally retained the lowest curve as increasingly stringent posterior thresholds were applied. The ancestry-weighted prior was the best of the three direct likelihood-based approaches. It was consistently more accurate than the Asian/Native American-only prior because it better represented the mixed ancestry of NA19663. The Asian/Native American-only curve was non-monotonic: removing its lower-confidence calls did not always improve discordance, indicating that this mismatched prior could produce confidently incorrect calls. The uniform prior improved steadily with increasing missingness but discarded more calls than the ancestry-weighted and Beagle methods to reach a similar discordance level.

Beagle performed best for NA19663. With all comparable sites called, its discordance rate was 4.53%, lower than 7.10% for the ancestry-weighted prior, 8.63% for the Asian/Native American prior, and 9.03% for the uniform prior. At a posterior threshold of 0.90, Beagle's discordance fell to 1.26%, while 12.83% of otherwise comparable sites became missing. Raising the threshold therefore improved accuracy among retained calls at the cost of genome coverage. The same trade-off occurred for every method, but Beagle retained the largest number of calls and achieved the lowest discordance at the 0.90 threshold.

## Calling rules and numerical safeguards {#calling-rules}

An exact posterior tie was resolved by choosing the lowest genotype code in the order 0, 1, 2. Before constructing Hardy-Weinberg priors, allele frequencies were restricted to the interval $[10^{-12}, 1-10^{-12}]$ to avoid a zero normalization denominator at boundary frequencies. Every posterior triplet, including the rounded probabilities written by Beagle, was normalized to sum to one before calling. These safeguards affect numerical stability and deterministic tie handling without materially changing non-boundary posterior probabilities.
