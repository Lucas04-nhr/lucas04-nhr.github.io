## Exercise 1: Transition matrices {#transition-matrices}

The observed sequence is:

```text
CCPCKCOORCCPPCKKCOCC
```

Its 20 symbols produce **19 adjacent transitions**. Rows represent the source state and columns the destination, both ordered **C, K, O, P, R**.

::: table align="center" title="Transition counts Aₖₗ"

| From / To | C | K | O | P | R | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| C | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 9 |
| K | 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 3 |
| O | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 3 |
| P | 2 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 3 |
| R | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |

:::

Normalize each row to obtain the transition probabilities:

$$
a_{kl}=\frac{A_{kl}}{\sum_j A_{kj}},\qquad
a=\begin{pmatrix}
1/3&2/9&2/9&2/9&0\\
2/3&1/3&0&0&0\\
1/3&0&1/3&0&1/3\\
2/3&0&0&1/3&0\\
1&0&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The highest estimated probability is **R → C**, with $a_{RC}=1$. It is supported by only one observed transition. The final C has no successor, so the C row uses a denominator of 9 rather than 10.

## Exercise 2: Suffixes and BWT {#suffixes-and-bwt}

For `ACGTCG$`, the suffixes in original position order are:

```text
ACGTCG$, CGTCG$, GTCG$, TCG$, CG$, G$, $
```

With `$` smaller than every letter, sorting the suffixes gives the following order. Appending the preceding prefix to each suffix constructs the corresponding cyclic rotation.

::: table align="center" title="Sorted suffixes and Burrows–Wheeler matrix"

| Row | Position | Suffix | Cyclic rotation | F | L |
|---:|---:|---|---|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | 7 | `$` | `$ACGTCG` | `$` | G |
| 2 | 1 | `ACGTCG$` | `ACGTCG$` | A | `$` |
| 3 | 5 | `CG$` | `CG$ACGT` | C | T |
| 4 | 2 | `CGTCG$` | `CGTCG$A` | C | A |
| 5 | 6 | `G$` | `G$ACGTC` | G | C |
| 6 | 3 | `GTCG$` | `GTCG$AC` | G | C |
| 7 | 4 | `TCG$` | `TCG$ACG` | T | G |

:::

Thus **F = `$ACCGGT`** and **BWT = L = `G$TACCG`**.

### Reconstruction by LF mapping {#lf-mapping}

Match the *nth occurrence* of a character in L to its *nth occurrence* in F. Subscripts below label occurrences from top to bottom; row numbers are one-based.

::: table align="center" title="Occurrence matching for ACGTCG$"

| Row | F | L | LF(row) |
|---:|:---:|:---:|---:|
| 1 | $ | G₁ | 5 |
| 2 | A₁ | $ | 1 |
| 3 | C₁ | T₁ | 7 |
| 4 | C₂ | A₁ | 2 |
| 5 | G₁ | C₁ | 3 |
| 6 | G₂ | C₂ | 4 |
| 7 | T₁ | G₂ | 6 |

:::

Start at row 2, where L contains `$`, and follow LF:

```text
Rows:       2 → 1 → 5 → 3 → 7 → 6 → 4 → 2
Read from L: $   G   C   T   G   C   A
```

LF moves backward through the sequence. Reversing `$GCTGCA` recovers **`ACGTCG$`**.

## Exercise 3: Reconstructing YO$OGF {#reconstructing-goofy}

Sorting **L = `YO$OGF`** gives **F = `$FGOOY`**.

::: table align="center" title="Occurrence matching for YO$OGF"

| Row | F | L | LF(row) |
|---:|:---:|:---:|---:|
| 1 | $ | Y₁ | 6 |
| 2 | F₁ | O₁ | 4 |
| 3 | G₁ | $ | 1 |
| 4 | O₁ | O₂ | 5 |
| 5 | O₂ | G₁ | 3 |
| 6 | Y₁ | F₁ | 2 |

:::

Starting at row 3:

```text
Rows:       3 → 1 → 6 → 2 → 4 → 5 → 3
Read from L: $   Y   F   O   O   G
```

Reverse `$YFOOG` to obtain **`GOOFY$`**. Removing the terminator gives **GOOFY**. Matching repeated characters by occurrence order is what makes the reconstruction unambiguous.
